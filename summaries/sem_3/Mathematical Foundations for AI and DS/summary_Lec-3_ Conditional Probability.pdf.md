## Summary
The lecture covers conditional probability, independent events, and related results. Conditional probability is defined as the probability of an event B occurring given that event A has occurred, denoted by P(B/A) and calculated as P(A∩B)/P(A), provided P(A) is not equal to 0. The product theorem of probability states that P(A∩B) = P(A) × P(B/A). The lecture also discusses independent events, where the occurrence of one event does not affect the occurrence of another. If two events A and B are independent, then P(B/A) = P(B), and the product theorem takes the form P(A∩B) = P(A) × P(B). The concept of independence can be extended to multiple events. The lecture also provides examples and proofs of various results related to conditional probability and independent events.

## Key Formulae & Equations
- P(B/A) = P(A∩B) / P(A)
- P(A∩B) = P(A) × P(B/A)
- P(A∩B) = P(A) × P(B) for independent events A and B
- P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A∩B) (addition theorem)
- P(A∪B/C) = P(A/C) + P(B/C) - P(A∩B/C)

## Important Points
1. Conditional probability is defined as P(B/A) = P(A∩B) / P(A), provided P(A) is not equal to 0.
2. The product theorem of probability states that P(A∩B) = P(A) × P(B/A).
3. Independent events are defined as events where the occurrence of one event does not affect the occurrence of another.
4. For independent events A and B, P(B/A) = P(B), and P(A∩B) = P(A) × P(B).
5. The concept of independence can be extended to multiple events.
6. If A and B are independent, then A and B (and similarly A and B) are also independent.
7. If A and B are independent, then so are A and B.
8. The formula for conditional probability can be used to solve problems involving conditional probability.

- P(A∪B/C) = P(A/C) + P(B/C) - P(A∩B/C) is a conditional version of the addition theorem.
